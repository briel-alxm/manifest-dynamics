What happens to value after it is created?

An idea can begin with a person, move through a conversation, enter a system, and eventually become part of the world. But as it travels, its origin can disappear.

Manifest Dynamics

A framework for understanding how value emerges through interaction, how it moves through systems, and how it loses or preserves its connection to the people and conditions that created it.

01 / Start here

Value is not only an output.

It is also an origin, an intention, a context, and a relationship.

Most systems are designed to preserve outputs: the idea, the product, the process, the result. They are much less reliable at preserving the relationship between the output and the origin that made it possible.

That creates a recurring pattern. Someone notices or creates something valuable. The value moves through a larger system. The system adopts, repeats, or builds on it. The original source becomes harder to see. The value survives, but the origin does not.

Manifest Dynamics studies what happens when an expression survives but its source does not.

04 / Core concepts

The formal terms, translated.

The theory uses precise language, but readers should not need to master the vocabulary before understanding the human problem. Each concept begins here in plain language.

Origin & agency

Where meaningful action begins

An origin is the deeper pattern of clarity or intention from which meaningful action begins. Agency is the ability to move that intention into the world. Agency is not only effort; it is effort with direction.

Substrate & friction

The conditions around movement

Every person and organization moves through a world full of possibilities and resistance. Some resistance is internal. Some is social, economic, technical, institutional, or historical.

Signal emergence

When an origin becomes an expression

An idea, product, decision, or creative work is not just an object. It is an expression of a particular origin, context, intention, and process.

Attribution lag

When recognition arrives late

Systems often recognize value after they have already absorbed it. The idea is adopted first; the source is identified later, partially, or not at all.

Foundational insight orphaning

When the idea survives but the source disappears

An idea can become part of the world while the person who first carried it is forgotten. The system benefits from the insight, but the relationship between the insight and its origin is not resolved.

Closure & stabilization

What keeps value connected

Closure occurs when a system preserves the relationship between what was created and where it came from. It can take the form of authorship, credit, compensation, legal protection, provenance, documentation, or community.
